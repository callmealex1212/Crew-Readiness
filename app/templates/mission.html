A check-in is a submitted session. The longest agreed gap is an illustrative assumption, not a rule. Someone missing a check-in does not mean anything is wrong: it is a reason for a person to look in. This section never changes the readiness suggestion above.

04If the ground cannot answer quickly

On a long mission, a message to the ground can take minutes each way and some hours have no link at all. The crew still has to decide who does a critical task. This is the situation the tool is built around, as a prototype.

QuestionWhat the tool doesWho decides
Did my own pattern change?Shows My status on the device, against my own earlier sessions only.Me
Should someone take a second look before the operation?Suggests a short check-in and says why, in plain words.The crew, or whoever the mission rules name
Is a new keyboard, glove or routine just my new normal?Lets a person accept it; the baseline then restarts.The person
Does anything leave the device?Nine timing numbers per session stay in the local database. No typed text, no network at runtime.Not applicable

Not tested: real crews, microgravity, gloves, shared tablets, or any real mission. The scenario above is fictional and the benchmark is simulated. This is not a medical or fitness-for-duty tool.
